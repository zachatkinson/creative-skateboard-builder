### What it builds

- **#131, side-by-side cards on every step.** Each step's cards put the part's picture beside its words, as the deck step's compact cards do (D-322), the picture sized to its part: about 5rem square for the square photos of trucks, wheels and bearings. No setting.
- **#132, thumbnails in the review.** Each row of the review gets its part's thumbnail, the same picture and rendition as its card. A row without a picture keeps the space.
- **#134, guided start.** Optional questions before the first step: shoe size, where they skate (street, park, bowl, cruising), and a budget. The answers give:
  - a deck width, from the kit's width guide (#114, D-350);
  - a wheel size, from a new table on the kit's Build tab of riding style to wheel size, with defaults from published wheel guides, that the shop can change;
  - three ready builds (budget, mid, premium), in stock and passing fit.

  Each recommendation says why in plain words. The start can be skipped at every point, by one button or by leaving an answer blank. The answers live only in the build's link.
- **#135, smart swaps.** On the review and on a picked card: "Same board for less" and a better part per slot. One press swaps in a part that fits the rest of the build, with the price difference shown, and one Undo puts the build back.
  - "For less" is the cheapest part in stock that fits.
  - "Better" follows the shop's ranking per slot, and falls back to price where a slot has none.
  - The server's fit engine checks every swap, as it checks a pick.
- **#136, compare saved builds.** Under My account's My builds, up to three saved builds side by side. The parts that differ and the price gap are marked in words as well as colour, at today's prices, with D-254's note where a part changed since the save. On a phone it's one build per column, with a scroll, and by keyboard it's a real table.
- **#137, rider picks and ratings in each step.**
  - **Rider picks:** the presets rider builds already use, shown as the rider's pick on that part's card.
  - **Ratings:** the part's star rating and review count. They come from WooCommerce's own product API, which Creative Reviews keeps right for its reviews (its SPEC, "Product meta").
  - **When nothing shows:** where the shop has ratings turned off (`wc_review_ratings_enabled()`), or a part has no reviews.
- **#138, the changed area's highlight.** After a pick, the part's area of the board is outlined for about 600 ms. Under reduced motion it shows and fades without movement, and nothing moves the page.
- **#141, no Product schema on a kit's page.** A browser test that a kit's page and the Builder block's page print no Product JSON-LD, with WooCommerce's structured data and with Yoast's (the `:yoast` variant). Where one does, the builder removes it through that plugin's filter.
- **Carried over:** #93 and #110, the flakes left open with what was learned, and #95, the canary, open by design.

### The public API it adds

Each goes in `docs/HOOKS.md` with `@since 1.3.0`, in `docs/SETTINGS.md` where it is a setting, and in the API snapshot:

- **In `_cbuild_settings`**, which a later release extends (D-354): `wheel_guide`, rows of a riding style and a wheel size range in millimetres, or the defaults. A kit saved before 1.3.0 has none and gets the defaults.
- **A part's meta:** `_cbuild_rank`, the shop's rank for the part in its slot, a whole number, higher being better, or none.
- **Filters**, each answering after the setting it starts from, as the 1.2.0 settings' do:
  - `cbuild_guided_start`, the recommendations from the answers;
  - `cbuild_swaps`, the swaps offered for a build;
  - `cbuild_part_rank`, a part's rank.
- **In the state route's answer:** the swaps offered for the build, each with its slot, part and price difference; and on the cards, the rider's pick and the rating, both additions, with no key renamed (`schema/state.json`).
- **The build's link:** the guided start's answers as `cbuild[_guide][…]` parameters, which change no other parameter.
- **My builds:** a compare view at a query string on its own endpoint. No new endpoint is registered.

No table is added, and nothing public is renamed or removed.

### Decisions I expect to record

From D-390. Under Zach's overnight authority, each is decided on researched practice, recorded, and listed for him:

- **#134:** the riding style table's defaults, from published wheel guides with sources. How three builds are chosen: the cheapest, the middle and the dearest complete builds that fit and are in stock, one part per slot from the guided width and wheel size. And what shows when the shop can't make three.
- **#135:** a part's rank is a number on its Part specs, not an ordered list on the kit, since a slot can hold hundreds of parts. A slot with no ranks ranks by price, as Zach set.
- **#137:** ratings come from WooCommerce's product API, not Creative Reviews' own meta. The plugin keeps WooCommerce's numbers right and publishes no read API. So ratings show wherever the shop's reviews do, with Creative Reviews or without it.
- **#131:** each slot's thumbnail size, from the first view's whole-card count on the three themes.
- **#141:** whether the kit's Product markup goes. The issue says it does, since the kit isn't a product a shopper buys alone. The test decides whether there is any to remove.

### Risks, by dimension

| Dimension | What could go wrong | What catches it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DRY and SOLID | A second fit or price path for swaps or the guided builds; the card's picture made twice for the review | Swaps and guided builds through the one fit engine and the cart's prices; one card picture template used by the review; `composer arch` |
| Anti-patterns | The recommendations or swaps decided in the browser; markup built in JavaScript for new card parts | The domain decides, the store renders server templates (CLAUDE.md's one engine, the markup rule); review |
| Security | Guided answers or a compare's build IDs trusted from the link; another shopper's saved build shown in a compare | Answers re-validated as picks are; compare only reads the signed-in shopper's own builds, an integration test with another's ID |
| Privacy | Guided answers stored; ratings expose reviewers | Answers live in the link only, nothing stored (PRIVACY.md says so); only the average and count are shown |
| Accessibility | Side-by-side cards that read in the wrong order; the compare table built from divs; the highlight the only sign of a change; swaps announced twice | Source order kept, the picture `alt=""`; a real table with headers; the build sentence still says the change; axe and the screen-reader checks in the browser suite |
| Internationalisation | "8.25″ for a size 10 shoe" concatenated; star ratings with a fixed decimal point; French guide text | Each reason one translatable pattern; numbers through `wc_format_localized_decimal`; fr-ca and rtl projects |
| SEO | A kit's page keeps a Product JSON-LD that misstates price; guided links indexed | #141's test; guided parameters are build parameters, so the build link's noindex rules (#88) cover them |
| Performance | Ratings or swaps add queries per card; the review's thumbnails load new images; the compare page reads every build | Ratings from the product objects the step already primes; swaps from the index, one query; the review uses renditions the cards loaded; `composer test:benchmark:integration` and a query count test |
| Testing | The rules tested only on the happy path; swaps that pass in tests but not with tax | Unit tests at every boundary of the wheel table and the width guide; integration for prices with tax and the shop's currency; Infection on the domain at 100 % |
| Upgrades and compatibility | A 1.2.0 container can't build a changed class; a 1.2.0 kit without `wheel_guide` breaks the start | D-208's with-ers; `npm run upgrade:all`; a test for a kit with no new keys; `RollbackTest` against 1.2.0's schemas |
| Resilience | A swap's part sells out between the offer and the press; a guided build loses a part to stock | The press goes through the fit engine and is refused as any pick is, with the build kept; three builds become as many as fit, never one that fails |
| UX and content | Cards side by side that show fewer whole cards; a guided start that feels mandatory; "better" that reads as a guess | The first view's whole-card counts per step and theme; Skip at every point; "better" names its reason (the shop's rank, or the price) |
